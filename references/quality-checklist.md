# Quality Checklist

Run this once before delivering substantial long-form content. Fix real problems; don't loop chasing perfection.

## Reader value

- [ ] Does this answer the reader's actual query?
- [ ] Is useful information introduced quickly, or does the reader wade through background first?
- [ ] Is there obvious filler that could be deleted without loss?

## Naturalness

- [ ] Does sentence rhythm vary — lengths, structures, openings?
- [ ] Are paragraph lengths natural rather than uniform?
- [ ] Is every transition earned (expressing a real relationship), with the empty ones cut?
- [ ] Is anything obviously formulaic — stock intro, triple-item lists everywhere, em dashes as default glue, "In conclusion"?

## Specificity

- [ ] Are vague claims replaced with concrete mechanisms, numbers, or conditions where possible?
- [ ] Are the examples relevant and realistic?
- [ ] Does the article explain how things work, not only that they're beneficial?

## SEO

- [ ] Is the dominant search intent satisfied by the structure and content?
- [ ] Is the primary topic unmistakable from the title, intro, and headings?
- [ ] Are headings descriptive enough to skim?
- [ ] Do keywords read naturally, with zero stuffed or awkward exact-match phrases?

## Accuracy

- [ ] Were any facts, statistics, or citations invented? (Must be none.)
- [ ] Were any user-supplied facts, numbers, names, URLs, or code accidentally changed? (Must be none.)
- [ ] Are conditional claims appropriately qualified — and confident claims actually supportable?

## Editing

- [ ] Is any idea repeated in different words?
- [ ] Is the introduction longer than it needs to be?
- [ ] Does the conclusion add value — or should it be cut entirely?
- [ ] Are there headings or lists that fragment prose without helping the reader?
